# Architecture — Dual-Camera High-Speed Carrier

**A worksheet, not a design.** Every line below is a question this board has to
answer, and none of them is answered here. Nothing in this file is a
recommendation, and the order of the sections carries no preference.

The questions were derived from [the brief](../BRIEF.md) and from what this
board is meant to stress in the benchmark:

- MIPI/serializer high-speed lanes
- compact connectors
- power filtering
- lane escape

Those are the places where a wrong answer shows up in copper.

Answer them in this file as the design is made, each answer carrying the
evidence that supports it, and record the corresponding choice against its
`OPEN-nn` entry in [board/requirements.md](../board/requirements.md). An answer
without evidence is a guess wearing a document's clothes — and this benchmark is
allowed to refuse an unsupported claim rather than invent one.

## Camera link architecture and interface choice

- Which realistic modern camera interface carries the two camera streams to the host — direct MIPI CSI-2, a serializer/deserializer link, or another modern camera interface — and what makes that the right call for this carrier?
- If a serializer/deserializer architecture is chosen, does the serializer sit on this carrier, and is the deserializer on the host side or also on this board?
- How are the two camera streams presented to the host: two independent links, one aggregated link, or a virtual-channel-multiplexed link?
- For every candidate device, does public layout guidance actually exist and is it accessible? (REQ-05 conditions part selection on this, so it is a gate on the candidate list rather than a preference.)
- What lane count and per-lane data rate does the chosen architecture require, and from which bandwidth assumptions does that number derive?
- What is the fallback if no usable public layout guidance can be found for the preferred device family?

## Camera module interface and mechanical mating

- What camera module class is targeted, and what is its electrical interface, pinout and supply set?
- How does each module attach — board-to-board connector, flex connector, or direct mount — and what does that choice cost in escape-routing area?
- What are the two cameras' relative positions and orientations, and is any baseline or co-planarity between them required by the intended use?
- How are camera-side connector pinouts assigned, and does that assignment let the high-speed pairs escape without crossing?
- What mechanical retention, strain relief or flex bend radius does the chosen mating scheme demand?

## Restrictive connector placement and high-speed lane escape

- Where are the camera connectors and the host connector placed, and what specifically makes that placement restrictive per REQ-10?
- For each high-speed pair, trace the escape path from connector pad to destination pin: how many layer changes, and where does each occur?
- Do any pairs have to cross under or around the other camera's connector, and how is that congestion resolved without breaking pair symmetry?
- What is the minimum pitch and pad geometry at the tightest connector, and what trace/space and via class does escaping it require?
- Are there escapes that only close if the layer count or via technology is upgraded — and is that upgrade being made honestly or assumed away?
- How is the escape region kept free of stubs, unreferenced spans and plane voids?

## Return paths, reference planes and stackup

- What is the layer stackup, and which reference plane does each high-speed layer use?
- For each layer transition on a high-speed pair, where is the return current going, and what stitching via or capacitor provides it?
- Are there any plane splits, and does any high-speed pair cross one? If so, what is the mitigation?
- What differential and single-ended impedance targets apply, what published source sets them, and how was the geometry solved to hit them?
- Is impedance control being requested from the fabricator, and at what tolerance?
- How does the compact outline constrain ground-pour continuity beneath the escape regions?

## Power architecture and camera rails

- What rail(s) enter the board, on which connector, and what is the total current budget across both cameras plus interface silicon?
- Which rails does each camera module require, and which of them are noise-sensitive (analog, PLL, PHY)?
- Is each rail generated by a switching regulator, a linear regulator, or passed through, and what drove each choice?
- Is any power-up or power-down sequencing required by the camera modules or the interface silicon, and what enforces it?
- How is regulator noise — of whatever kind the chosen topology produces — kept out of the high-speed lanes and away from the camera analog supplies in a compact outline?
- Where do the regulators and their input/output loops sit relative to the escape regions and the connectors?

## Power filtering and decoupling

- What decoupling scheme is applied at each camera connector, and is there physical room for it given the restrictive placement?
- Are ferrites, pi filters or separate regulators used on sensitive camera supplies, and what evidence supports the values chosen?
- What is the target impedance for each rail across the frequency band of interest, and how is it verified rather than asserted?
- If any rail is generated by a switching regulator, how are its hot loops laid out so their fields do not couple into the lane escape region?
- Does any filtering choice introduce a rail droop or sequencing hazard at camera power-on inrush?

## Clocking and camera control plane

- Where do the camera clocks originate — on-board oscillator, host, or recovered from the link — and what jitter budget applies?
- What control bus reaches the cameras, and how are two devices addressed or arbitrated on it?
- What sideband signals (reset, power-down, strobe, sync, interrupt) are needed, and who drives them?
- Do the two cameras need to be frame-synchronised, and if so what carries the sync and what is its skew budget?
- How are control and clock nets routed so they do not couple into the high-speed pairs in the congested escape area?

## ESD and exposed-interface protection

- Which nets on this board count as exposed, and on what reasoning is that boundary drawn? The brief requires 'ESD where exposed' and defines neither term.
- What ESD stress level is the board being designed to survive, and on what basis was that level chosen?
- For high-speed lanes, what capacitance budget can a protection device consume without degrading the link, and does the candidate meet it?
- Where is protection placed relative to the connector so the discharge path does not run across sensitive circuitry?
- How does protection placement interact with the already-tight escape region — does it fit, or does it force a placement change?
- What protects the power and control pins, as distinct from the high-speed lanes?

## Host connector and system integration

- What is the host connector, and what does it carry — high-speed lanes only, or power, control and clocks as well?
- What is the assumed cable or mating board on the other side, and what channel loss does that add to the link budget?
- How is the host connector's pinout assigned to give clean pair escapes and adequate ground return pins?
- What keying or polarity protection prevents mis-mating?
- Does the host connector's mechanical position conflict with the camera connectors' escape needs, and how is that conflict resolved?

## Manufacturability, DFM and fabrication class

- What minimum trace/space, drill and annular ring does the design actually require, and does the intended fabricator support it?
- Are microvias, blind or buried vias being used, what escape problem does each solve, and what fabrication-class and cost consequences follow?
- Do the compact fine-pitch connectors have adequate courtyard clearance and paste/stencil provision for reliable assembly?
- Are components placed such that both camera connectors and the host connector are accessible for rework?
- What fiducials, panelisation and test-point provisions are included, given how little free surface the compact outline leaves?

## Verification, bring-up and evidence

- What simulation or field-solver results back the impedance and skew numbers, and are they recorded in the repository?
- How will the high-speed links be probed during bring-up given the restrictive connector placement?
- What is the bring-up order, what dependency between subsystems determines it, and what indicates success at each step?
- Which public layout-guidance rules were followed, from which sources, and where is each rule cited against the actual routing?
- What would prove that both camera streams reach the host correctly, and what test setup is needed?
